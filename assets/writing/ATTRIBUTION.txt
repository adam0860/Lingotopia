Stroke-order data used by Writing practice
==========================================

assets/writing/ja_strokes.json  (Japanese kanji, hiragana, katakana)
  Derived from KanjiVG, Copyright (C) 2009-2011 Ulrich Apel.
  http://kanjivg.tagaini.net
  Licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0
  https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/
  Changes: stroke paths were flattened to centre-line polylines on a
  1000 x 1000 grid and simplified (tools/build_writing_data.py).
  Share-alike: this derived file is distributed under the same licence.

assets/writing/zh_strokes.json  (Chinese characters)
  Derived from hanzi-writer-data 2.0.1 (https://github.com/chanind/hanzi-writer-data),
  which is built from Make Me a Hanzi (https://github.com/skishore/makemeahanzi)
  and Arphic PL KaitiM GB / Arphic PL UKai stroke data.
  Licensed under the Arphic Public License; see ARPHICPL.txt.
  Changes: stroke median lines converted to a 1000 x 1000 grid, y down
  (tools/build_writing_data.py).

assets/writing/ja_readings.json  (kanji readings for the Japanese writing practice)
  Derived from KANJIDIC2, Copyright (C) the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group
  (https://www.edrdg.org/wiki/index.php/KANJIDIC_Project), as bundled in the jamdict-data package.
  Licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0
  https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/
  Changes: on and kun readings and English meanings only; suffix forms and duplicates removed
  (tools/build_writing_readings.py).

assets/writing/zh_readings.json  (pinyin and meanings for the Chinese writing practice)
  Pinyin from pypinyin (MIT licence) cross-checked with CC-CEDICT, Copyright (C) MDBG
  (https://cc-cedict.org), as bundled in the hanzipy package.
  Licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0
  https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/
  Changes: tone-marked pinyin (up to 3 readings) and up to 3 short English glosses per character
  (tools/build_writing_readings_zh.py).

assets/writing/ko_strokes.json, ko_readings.json  (Hangul writing practice)
  Authored for Lingotopia: the stroke lines of each Hangul letter are composed into syllable blocks
  (tools/build_hangul_strokes.py). The stroke order of each letter was checked against the open
  hangeul-stroke-order diagrams (https://github.com/MagisterAdamus/hangeul-stroke-order,
  Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0); no artwork from that set is included.
  Romanization follows the Revised Romanization of Korean for the syllable on its own.
